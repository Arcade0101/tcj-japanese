キャスト — the three voices

Who these people are, how they talk, and what to listen for when casting them. Written for auditioning voices: "a Japanese male voice" is useless direction — "calm, unflappable, the person you'd want to call when you're running late" is something you can actually judge a sample against.

The host — English only · he frames the skit

Ben Narrator — sets up the skit, doesn't teach it English only

Role
Bookends the skit. He opens by saying who's in it and what to listen for, steps back entirely for the Japanese, then closes with a one-line recap.
Drives by
Giving the listener something to listen for — not a personal story, a listening prompt: who's talking, what's going on, what to pay attention to.
Personality
Plain, clear, welcoming. No verbal tics or asides — the point is to get out of the way before the skit starts.
Speech
Short, direct sentences. States the situation, then hands off: "Alright, let's listen."
Never
Speaks Japanese. Announces a grammar point or vocabulary word. Explains what a line meant.
Casting Male, warm but mature — measured, pleasant to listen to for the whole episode, a little gravel in it. English only, so the delivery carries the whole episode's personality on its own instead of splitting it across two hosts.

The skit — Japanese · they perform

Age
Twenties. Working and studying — the situations the listener is actually in.
Role
The one with the problem. She's late, she's unwell, she needs to ask for something. Every episode's grammar arrives through her needing it.
Personality
Lively, polite, a little flustered but entirely competent. Apologises properly and moves on.
Speech
Natural polite Japanese with the hesitations real speech has — あの… · 実は… · はい……
Never
Speaks English. Narrates. Explains grammar. Reads romaji.
Casting ja-JP female, twenties. Natural and lively. Must survive sounding slightly stressed without turning shrill. Reject anything that reads like a station announcement — the test line is a phone call, not a broadcast.

どうしが — how they work together

Ben frames, the skit shows. He opens by naming who's talking and what to listen for, steps back entirely for the skit, then closes with a one-line recap of what happened. He never explains a grammar point or names a pattern — nothing is taught directly, the skit teaches itself.

Haru needs something, Yuki responds. Every skit is a small transaction — an apology, a request, a piece of bad news. The grammar of the week is whatever that transaction requires.

The layers never mix. Ben and the skit characters exist in different layers: he talks about the skit, never to it, and he's English-only where they're Japanese-only. The casting is fixed for the series — Haru is always the female Japanese voice, Yuki always the male — but what they are to each other changes per episode: colleagues here, classmates or friends later. Write the relationship into the skit, never into the voice.
